# Audience: PM peer or your team

> Template. Copy this to `<name>.md` and edit the four sections marked with EDIT.

## Role

EDIT: A PM at your level, your direct team, or a cross-functional partner of similar seniority. Examples: PM peer, design partner, your direct reports.

## What they care about

1. Substance. What did you decide, what's the trade-off, what changed.
2. Real numbers. Not status colors, not "going well", actual data.
3. What's blocking you and what you've already tried.
4. Specific asks. "Looking for a read on X" beats "thoughts welcome".
5. Time efficiency. They're busy. Get to the point.

## Default framing

Direct and casual. Use their first name if it's a known peer. Skip context they already share with you.

Default spine: any of A/B/C/D as fits. PM peers can handle all four.

Exec summary lens (3 cards) is optional for peer decks — sometimes you can skip the exec_summary entirely and go cover → agenda → content for short peer reviews.

## Content cuts

- **Include**: what you decided, why, what's blocking, what you've tried, what you want their read on.
- **Cut**: corporate-speak, throat-clearing intros ("As you may recall..."), repeated context.
- **Translate**: only if the acronym is truly from a different workstream they don't touch.

## Tone adjustments

PM-to-PM. Casual register OK. Contractions OK. Direct opinions OK ("I think we should..."). Real talk on what's hard.

Avoid: false polish. PM peers can smell pitch-deck language from a mile.

## Slide-level defaults

| Slide type | Default behavior |
|---|---|
| cover | Subtitle is "for <peer's name>, quick review" or similar |
| exec_summary | Optional. If used, lead is your honest take, not a polished claim. |
| do_dont | Great fit for PM peers — they can compare your DO/DON'T to what they'd do |
| section_divider | Plain English |
| close slide | "Looking for your read on X" or "What would you do here" — open the dialogue |

## Things they will ask

1. What's your honest take?
2. What's the trade-off you're worried about?
3. Have you talked to <other PM> about this?
4. What's the smallest version that ships?
5. Is this on the roadmap or is it net-new?

## Things to NEVER do for this audience

- Polish the deck more than the conversation warrants. A 3-slide back-of-napkin beats a 12-slide polished pitch for peer review.
- Hide what's hard. They'll ask anyway and the deflection costs trust.
- Default to "going well" status. Real numbers or no numbers.
- Use the deck as a substitute for a 10-minute conversation if the conversation would be faster.

## When this is the wrong audience config

- If the peer is presenting up the chain after your review, build for THEIR audience, not theirs. Ask which.
- If you need a formal decision from leadership, this template won't have the polish; use one of the senior-audience templates.
