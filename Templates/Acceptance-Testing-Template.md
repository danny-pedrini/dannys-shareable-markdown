## Appendix

<!-- OMIT IN BOTH MODES. Delete the whole appendix. -->

### When in the process should we use it?

- **End of development:** to validate whether a feature meets its requirements.
- **Pre-release:** to confirm readiness for the next launch gate.
- **Stakeholder validation:** to confirm the system delivers on business goals and user expectations.

### What it is not

- **Early stages of development.** During prototyping or proof of concept, where rapid iteration matters more than a fixed bar.
- **For undefined requirements.** If requirements are vague, acceptance testing is premature. Fix the requirements first.
- **For discovering unknown issues.** Use exploratory or system testing to find unexpected problems.
- **For measuring AI output quality.** That belongs to the eval workstream. See the scope section above.

### What it should include

- **Clear description.** What the feature is expected to do.
  - Example: "The provider can accept a recommendation and reach the prescribe modal in one click."
- **Conditions of success.** Measurable and testable.
  - Example: "When the chart is incomplete, task creation is held and no provider task is created."
- **Functional and non-functional requirements.** Both what the system does and how well it performs.
  - Functional: "The patient's answer is written to the chart, attributed and dated."
  - Non-functional: "The center pane renders the chart and its recommendation as one atomic view."
- **Edge cases and constraints.** Named explicitly so nothing is ambiguous.
  - Example: "A patient who has not consented is not asked via Ivy; the task proceeds to the provider as it does today."
- **User scenarios.** Concrete examples of how someone will interact with the system.
  - Example: "As a provider, I open a held task after the chart completes and see the patient's answers in the center pane."
